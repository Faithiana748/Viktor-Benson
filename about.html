The Story of Viktor Benson

I'm Viktor Benson — a content creator, entrepreneur, digital marketer, and Chemical Engineering professional. My story isn't really about making money online. It's about learning how to create value, build an audience, lose things I worked hard to build, and start again.

100K+ YouTube subscribers reached
$100K+ generated across ventures
2019 started creating on a basic phone

Where It Started

I grew up in a humble home where money was not something we had in abundance. I saw firsthand what it meant for people to work hard and still struggle financially — one story in particular, of how much effort could go into a job while the reward stayed extremely limited, has stayed with me.

That shaped one of my earliest beliefs: there has to be another way. I didn't know exactly what that way was.

Like many young people, I followed the conventional path at first — Computer Engineering at diploma level, then Chemical Engineering, without having everything figured out about what my future would look like. I've always believed in education, which is why I'm currently pursuing a Master's degree in Chemical Engineering alongside everything else. But at the same time, I was becoming increasingly curious about something outside the traditional career path: the internet.

My First Experiments With Business

Before I understood content creation as a business, I was already experimenting with entrepreneurship. Between 2017 and 2019, I was importing and selling shoes — not a sophisticated business, but it taught me something important: I enjoyed finding an opportunity, figuring out how it worked, and turning it into something bigger.

I wasn't simply looking for a job. I was looking for leverage — a way to understand how one person could reach thousands or even millions of people without a traditional company, office, or large workforce. That curiosity eventually led me to YouTube.

Discovering Content Creation

In 2019, I started creating content on YouTube — recording videos on a basic Tecno phone with a poor screen, dealing with power supply issues, with no team, no producer, and no one teaching me how to build a media business. I was learning by doing, experimenting with cryptocurrency content, screen recordings, and tutorials on whatever I believed people would find useful.

At the beginning, the numbers were small. But I kept publishing, and eventually people started watching. One of my channels reached more than 100,000 subscribers and generated around $3,000 a month. But the biggest lesson wasn't the money — it was discovering the power of an audience.

Then I Lost It All

The YouTube channel I had spent years building was deleted after a policy mistake. Just like that, something I'd worked incredibly hard for disappeared — the income, the audience, the momentum, all of it. Going from thousands of dollars a month to essentially zero was difficult.

You can lose a platform. You can lose a business. You can lose a product. But the skills you develop while building them can stay with you.

I had learned how to create content, get attention, understand an audience, market, sell, and build communities. Those skills didn't disappear when the channel did. So I started again.

The Years of Experimentation

The years that followed were filled with experimentation — more channels, some that worked and some that didn't, across cryptocurrency, affiliate marketing, CPA marketing, YouTube, TikTok, X, digital products, freelancing, and other internet-based businesses. Across all of it, I've generated more than $100,000, but the journey was far from linear.

At one point I realized I was spending too much energy trying to recreate previous successes. I'd built multiple things, but not necessarily one thing that could compound for decades. That realization changed the direction of my life and my business.

The Lesson: Build an Audience, Not Just a Business

The specific business models changed. The platforms changed. The products changed. But one thing consistently created opportunities: an audience. That led me to the principle I now use in almost everything I do.

Value + Audience = Opportunity.

You can be extremely knowledgeable and have no audience. You can have an audience and provide no value. But combine real value with an audience that trusts you, and opportunities begin to compound. That's why content creation became the foundation of everything I build.

Building Coursearly

Coursearly is one of the biggest expressions of that philosophy — a platform where creators can upload and sell digital products and courses. Create once, upload once, keep earning from your knowledge. I wanted to build something particularly relevant to creators and entrepreneurs in Africa who have knowledge, skills, and experience they can package into digital products, without needing to figure out a website, hosting, payments, and delivery from scratch.

But Coursearly isn't just about selling courses — it's infrastructure for turning what anyone knows into something valuable, whether that's design, trading, cooking, running ads, or years of professional experience.

Building MyBlud

Alongside Coursearly, I began building MyBlud, built around another lesson I learned through content creation: people don't only want information, they want community. You can watch a video and learn something, buy a course and gain knowledge — but there's another layer that matters when people are trying to accomplish something: being surrounded by others on the same journey.

MyBlud gives creators, entrepreneurs, educators, and community leaders a way to bring people together around shared goals. Content attracts people, an audience creates relationships, courses package knowledge, and communities deepen those relationships. The pieces are connected.

What I Have Learned From Failure

Success can sometimes teach you less than failure. When something you spent years building disappears, you're forced to separate yourself from the thing you built, and ask what you actually learned and what you can rebuild.

Losing channels taught me not to depend entirely on a platform. Building businesses taught me that not every opportunity deserves my attention. Making money online taught me that revenue and sustainability aren't the same thing. Building Coursearly taught me that building for other creators means understanding their problems, not just building what I personally want.

What Viktor Benson Is Becoming

Today, I'm building ViktorBenson.com as the home of my personal brand — not just a biography and a list of achievements, but a documented journey of building companies, creating content, pursuing education, making mistakes, and rebuilding. The platforms will change. The businesses will change. The technology will change.

Create value. Build an audience. Earn trust. Use that trust to create opportunities.

My mission is to help people understand the power of content creation and entrepreneurship — not by telling them what worked for me, but by documenting the wins, the failures, the experiments, and the lessons along the way. My long-term vision is a personal brand that compounds for decades, with Coursearly helping people turn knowledge into products, MyBlud helping people turn audiences into communities, and this site bringing it all together.

I don't consider my story finished. There are still businesses to build, mistakes to make, and ideas I haven't executed. But the difference now is that I understand what I'm building — a body of work, an audience, and businesses, one step at a time. This is the story behind Viktor Benson, and it's still being written.
